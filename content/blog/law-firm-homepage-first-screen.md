The first screen of your homepage is the part a visitor sees before they scroll. On a phone, that is not much room. Many people decide in that space whether to keep reading or go back to the search results.

You do not need to fit everything there. You need three things to be clear.

## 1. What kind of help you offer

Say what the firm does in words a non-lawyer uses. "Estate planning and probate for families" is clearer than a general line about dedication and excellence. If the firm handles a few related areas, name them plainly. If it handles many, name the group of people you help, such as "small businesses" or "people facing a criminal charge."

A good test: could a visitor repeat back what you do after a few seconds? If the answer depends on reading a paragraph, the headline is doing too little.

## 2. Where you practice

Most people want a lawyer who can help them where they live. Put the city, county, or state near the top. A short line such as "Serving Dayton and the Miami Valley" answers the question before the visitor has to look for it.

If the firm has more than one office, name the main area in the first screen and list the offices further down the page or on their own pages.

## 3. How to reach you

Show one clear way to start a conversation. That is usually a phone number that can be tapped on a phone and a button that leads to a short contact form. Use a plain label such as "Call the office" or "Send a message."

Keep it to one or two options. A row of five buttons makes the visitor choose before they are ready.

## What can wait

These are worth having on the site, but they do not need to compete for the first screen:

- The firm's history
- A long list of every matter type
- Bar memberships and awards
- News, articles, and blog posts

Move them lower on the page or onto their own pages. They still help the visitor who wants more. They just do not crowd the visitor who only needs the basics.

## A quick way to check your own homepage

Open your site on your phone. Do not scroll. Ask someone who does not know the firm three questions:

1. What does this firm help with?
2. Where is it?
3. How would you contact it?

If they can answer all three from what is on the screen, the first screen is doing its job. If they hesitate on one, that is the line to rewrite.

Keep the wording within your state's attorney advertising rules. A clear description of the work is enough. It does not need a promise about the outcome.
