People visit the about page to find out who they would be working with. They want a sense of the person, not a list of adjectives. An honest, specific page does more than a page full of big claims, and it is easier to keep within your state's advertising rules.

## Start with who the firm helps

Open with a sentence or two about the people the firm works with and the kind of help it offers. This connects the about page to the reason the visitor came.

## Introduce the people

For each lawyer, include:

- **A real, recent photo.** Not a stock image.
- **Name and role.**
- **Bar admissions.** The states and courts where they are admitted.
- **Education.** Law school and year, if they want to share it.
- **The work they do now.** In plain language.
- **A line or two that is personal**, if they are comfortable. Why they practice in this area, or the community they are part of.

If staff are the first people a caller talks to, introducing them helps too.

## Be specific, not superlative

Specific facts build more trust than broad claims. "Has practiced estate planning in Ohio since 2012" says more than "highly experienced and dedicated."

Avoid words that claim to be the top choice, or that compare the firm to others. Avoid describing a lawyer in a way that suggests a formal certification they do not hold. State rules on this vary, and some restrict particular terms. When in doubt, describe what the lawyer does rather than giving them a label.

## Awards and memberships

Bar associations and community groups are fine to list. For awards and ratings, many states have rules about how they can be shown, sometimes including a disclaimer. Check your state's rules before adding them, and keep the list short.

## Results and reviews

The about page is not the place for case results or client quotes. Many states regulate how those can be used in lawyer advertising. If you do use them anywhere on the site, follow your state's rules, and never invent or reword them.

## Close with a next step

End with a short line inviting the visitor to call or send a message, and link to the contact page. Someone who has just read about the people is often ready to talk to one of them.

## Keep it current

Update the page when someone joins or leaves, when photos get old, or when admissions change. An out-of-date about page quietly undermines the trust it is meant to build.
