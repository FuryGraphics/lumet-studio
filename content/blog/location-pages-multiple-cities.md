If your firm serves more than one city, visitors from each place want to know you can help them where they are. A location page for each office or main service area answers that question directly.

The goal is a page that helps someone from that city. A page that only swaps the city name into the same paragraph does not.

## When a location page makes sense

A separate page usually makes sense when:

- The firm has an office in that city, or
- The firm regularly works with people there and appears in the local courts or agencies

If the firm only occasionally works in a place, mentioning it on a service-area page is often more honest than giving it a page of its own.

## What to put on each page

- **The office details**, if there is an office: address, phone number, hours, and parking or entrance notes.
- **Who the office helps.** The practice areas handled from that location, in plain words.
- **Local details that are true and useful.** Which courthouse matters are usually heard in, for example, or whether meetings can happen by video.
- **Who works there**, if specific lawyers or staff are based at that office.
- **A map or directions**, if people visit.
- **A clear way to get in touch.** The local phone number and a contact form.

## What to avoid

- Copying the same text onto every city page and changing only the name. Visitors notice, and it does not help them.
- Listing an address where the firm does not actually meet people. That can mislead visitors, and Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) do not allow virtual offices on a Business Profile.
- Implying an office in a city where there is none. Many state bars have rules about how a firm describes its office locations. Check yours.

## Match the Google Business Profile

Each real office can have its own Google Business Profile. The name, address, and phone number on the location page should match that profile exactly. Link the profile's website field to the matching location page, not just the homepage.

## Link the pages together

Add a short list of locations in the footer or on a "Locations" page. From each location page, link to the practice-area pages that apply there. A visitor who lands on the wrong city should be able to find the right one in one tap.

## Keep them current

When an office moves, changes hours, or closes, update the location page and the Google Business Profile at the same time. An old address on one page and a new one on another confuses people trying to find you.
