Many people look for a lawyer on their phone, sometimes while dealing with the problem that sent them looking. A site that is hard to read or tap on a small screen makes a stressful moment harder.

You can check most of this yourself in a few minutes.

## Read it on your own phone

Open the site on your phone, not just a desktop browser made narrow. Try it on a slow connection if you can. Look at the homepage, one practice-area page, and the contact page.

## Text size and contrast

- Body text should be easy to read without zooming.
- Lines should not run edge to edge with no margin.
- Light gray text on a white background is hard to read in daylight. Choose a darker color.

## Tap targets

Buttons and links need enough room to tap without hitting the one next to them. Watch for:

- Menu items packed close together
- Small text links in the footer
- A phone number that is not a tappable link

## The menu

On a phone, most sites tuck the menu behind an icon. That is fine. Keep the menu short and make sure it closes easily. Put "Contact" in it, and keep the phone number visible outside the menu if you can.

## Forms

Forms are where phones cause the most trouble.

- Use the right field types so the phone shows a number pad for phone numbers and an email keyboard for email addresses.
- Keep labels visible above each field.
- Make the submit button large and clearly labeled.
- Fill in the form yourself and send it. Make sure the confirmation shows and the message arrives.

## Pop-ups and chat windows

A pop-up that covers the screen as soon as the page loads is hard to close on a phone. So is a chat window that hides the content. If the site uses either, make sure it is small, easy to dismiss, and does not cover the phone number or the menu.

## Speed

Large images are the most common reason a site loads slowly on a phone. Ask whoever maintains the site to size images for the web. Google's free [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) tool shows how a page performs on mobile and lists what is slowing it down.

## Accessibility goes together with this

Many of the same changes that help on a phone also help people who use screen readers or need larger text. The W3C's [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) are the common public reference.

## A short checklist

1. Can you read the homepage without zooming?
2. Can you tap the phone number and start a call?
3. Can you open and close the menu easily?
4. Can you fill in and send the contact form?
5. Does anything cover the content that you cannot dismiss?

If any answer is no, start there.
