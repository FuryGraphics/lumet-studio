Reviews on a Google Business Profile help people get a sense of a firm before they call. Many satisfied people would leave one if asked. The trick is asking in a way that feels like an invitation, not a request for a favor.

## Know the rules first

Google's [Prohibited and restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) for Maps reviews covers what businesses may and may not do. In short:

- You may encourage people to leave honest reviews.
- You may not offer anything in return for a review, such as a discount or a gift.
- You may not ask only the people you expect to be happy. Asking everyone in the same way is the safe approach.
- You may not pressure people, set staff quotas, or ask reviewers to say particular things.

Your state's attorney advertising and professional conduct rules may also apply to how a firm asks for and uses reviews. Check them, and check how they treat responding to reviews, before you start.

## Choose a natural moment

A good time to ask is usually when a matter wraps up and the person has said they are glad it is done. Asking in the middle of a matter, or right after a hard conversation, can feel out of place.

Ask consistently. If the firm sends a closing letter or email, the review request can be one short line at the end of it, sent to everyone.

## Keep the request short

A message like this works for most firms:

> Thank you for trusting us with this. If you're comfortable sharing your experience, a short Google review helps other people find the office. Here is the link. There's no need to include details about your matter.

That last sentence matters. It reminds the person that they do not have to share anything private.

## Make it easy

Google Business Profile gives the firm a link that opens the review form directly. The [Google Business Profile Help Center](https://support.google.com/business/) explains where to find it. Put that link in the message so the person does not have to search for the firm.

## Responding to reviews

Thank people briefly for positive reviews. Keep responses general.

Be careful with every response, positive or negative. Confirming that someone was a client, or discussing their matter, can raise confidentiality concerns even when the reviewer brought it up first. A short, general reply is usually the safer choice. If a review is negative, take time before answering, and consider whether your state bar has published guidance on this.

## What not to do

- Do not write reviews for the firm or ask friends and family to.
- Do not ask for reviews only from people who seem pleased.
- Do not copy reviews onto the website without checking your state's rules on using them in advertising.

A steady trickle of honest reviews, asked for politely and the same way each time, is the goal.
