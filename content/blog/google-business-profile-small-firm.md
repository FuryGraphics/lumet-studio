When someone searches for a law firm near them, the Google Business Profile is often the first thing they see. It shows the firm's name, address, hours, phone number, and reviews. For a small firm, a tidy profile is one of the simplest things to get right.

Google publishes the rules in [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177). This post walks through the parts that come up most for small firms. When this post and Google's page differ, follow Google's page. It changes from time to time.

## First, find out what already exists

Search for the firm's name and for each lawyer's name. You may find a profile you did not create, an old profile from a previous address, or a separate profile for a lawyer who has left. Note what you find before changing anything.

If a profile exists and nobody at the firm controls it, Google has a process for requesting ownership. The [Google Business Profile Help Center](https://support.google.com/business/) explains the steps.

## The name

Google's guidelines say the name should match the name you use in the real world, on your sign, your website, and your letterhead. Do not add practice areas, city names, or phone numbers to the name field. "Smith Law Office" is fine. "Smith Law Office Divorce Lawyer Columbus" is not.

## Firm profiles and lawyer profiles

Google allows individual practitioners, including lawyers, to have their own profiles in some cases. The guidelines cover how this works:

- When several lawyers practice at one location, the firm has one profile for that location, and each lawyer's profile uses only their own name.
- A solo lawyer practicing under a firm brand shares one profile, named in the format the guidelines describe.
- A lawyer should not create several profiles to cover different practice areas.

Read that section of the guidelines before creating anything new. Duplicate profiles are a common cleanup task.

## Address or service area

Use the real address of an office where you meet people during the hours you list. Google's guidelines say P.O. boxes, remote mailboxes, and virtual offices are not eligible.

If the firm does not meet people at its office, the guidelines describe a service-area setup where the address is hidden and a service area is shown instead.

## The rest of the details

- **Phone number.** The number the office answers. Keep it the same as the number on the website.
- **Website.** Link to the homepage or to the page for that office.
- **Hours.** The hours someone can actually reach the office. Update holiday hours before the holiday.
- **Categories.** Choose the category that describes the firm most accurately. Add others only if they apply.
- **Description.** A few plain sentences about who the firm helps and where. Keep it within your state's attorney advertising rules, the same as the website.
- **Photos.** A real photo of the office entrance or sign helps people find you. Avoid stock images.

## Keep it consistent

The name, address, and phone number should match across the profile, the website footer, and the firm's other listings. Small differences, like an old suite number, are worth fixing.

## Check it a few times a year

Profiles can change. Google may accept suggested edits from the public, and details drift as the firm changes. Put a reminder on the calendar to look at the profile, confirm the hours and phone number, and read any new reviews.
